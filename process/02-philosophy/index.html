On tending

Twelve houses, twelve clocks. The grammar of attention is not uniform across them — and the home screen is what makes the asymmetry visible.

Premise

The flattening

We treat all activity as if it were the same kind of thing — tasks performed in units of time. But the twelve houses do not share a temporal grammar.

A breath does not work like a friendship. A tidied room does not work like a mastered concept. A walk in the forest does not work like a paid invoice. To assemble these twelve under one verb — complete — is to flatten what makes each of them what it is.

This page is the place to set down, before any code is written, what we mean by time, by action, by tending. It is an inventory of asymmetries the app must respect.

A todo app that nudges you to complete an Animus item by Friday is not helping you with Animus. It is converting Animus into Domus.
I — The wheel

What the atrium is, before it is a screen

The atrium is structurally a wheel. Twelve names arranged in order around an open center, as in the twelve houses of a classical chart. The order moves from self outward to world — from Corpus at the top to Ludus closing the circle. Body, soul, time, dwelling, means, work, made-things, knowing, kinship, polity, wildness, play.

Fig. 1 — the twelve houses, self at the apex, world at the foot

The wheel is not a metaphor for the app. It is what the app is. The home screen is the wheel, rendered vertically because that is what fits a phone, but cosmologically a circle. You stand in the center; the rooms open in every direction.

This matters for a small reason and a large one. The small reason: the order is structural, not arbitrary, and must be preserved. The large reason: the user is at the center of their own attention, and the twelve are the bearings by which they orient. Atrium is a compass that happens to also keep lists.

Two notes on the diagram

Most houses are not points but ranges. Corpus is the breath you take right now and the body you inhabit at fifty. Both are correctly called Corpus. The house contains its scales. A daily-task framing addresses the leftmost end of each bar and pretends the rest does not exist.

The widest bars are the most fragile. The houses that span instant to lifetime — Vinculum, Ars, Corpus — are the ones most easily mis-tended by a tool built around days. They cannot be made legible at the day scale alone; the weekly conversation is what gives them a place to live at their proper scales.

III — The asymmetry of action

What you do is not what changes

The relationship between effort and effect differs across the twelve. Four shapes recur.

Linear
action →outcome ↑
Action and outcome scale together. Tidy the room, the room is tidier.
→ Domus · Patrimonium
Compounding
action →outcome ↑
Small consistent acts produce nothing for a long time, then everything.
→ Studium · Ars · Opus
Presence-only
presence →outcome ↑
No single act causes it. The outcome emerges only from steadiness.
→ Animus · Vinculum · Polis
Inverse
action →outcome ↑
Effort degrades the thing. The forest does not yield to effort.
→ Naturum · Ludus · Tempus

Fig. 3 — four shapes of action and outcome

A tool that treats these four as the same kind of “task to complete” gets at most one of them right. The list pane in each house honors the first shape, Linear. The conversation is the room in which the other three have a chance to register.

IV — Modes of tending

Five verbs of attention

Each house has a dominant mode. Some have two. The verb is not interchangeable.

Doing Direct execution
The thing is done or it isn’t. Action transfers cleanly to outcome. The list pane was built for this.
Domus · Patrimonium
Practicing Disciplined repetition
A single repetition produces nothing visible. A year of repetitions produces a different person.
Ars · Studium · Opus · Corpus
Tending Attention as the medium
Small consistent gestures whose value is in the gesture itself, not the result. Bursts of intensity do not substitute.
Vinculum · Animus
Showing up Presence in a setting
The setting does its work on you. Your only job is to be inside it long enough.
Naturum · Polis
Releasing Non-action, allowance
Outcome arises from putting down agenda. The clearest sign you are doing it well is that nothing is being achieved.
Ludus · Tempus

Fig. 4 — the five modes and their houses

Why this matters for the build

The list pane is a Doing surface. It will read every house as if it were Domus. This is fine — Doing is one of the five real modes — but it is not enough. The conversation pane is what makes room for the other four.

The LLM is not a coach. Its job is to honor the verb of the room it is in. In Vinculum it tends. In Studium it practices alongside. In Naturum it asks what the user saw, not what they planned. The role instruction in each system prompt is the thing that turns the same model into the right partner for each verb.

V — The container

Why the week, not the day

A day is too short to register Compounding and Tending — nothing has had time to take. A month is too long to register Doing — the room got messy and clean and messy and clean five times in the interval. The week is the only container in which all five modes leave a legible trace.

The lazy rollover matters here too. The week does not end because something happens at 23:59. It ends because the next time you return is the only moment at which the closing is meaningful — when there is a fresh page to lay against the old one and ask: what carries?

The summary is the residue of one week’s tending. meta_md is the slow accumulation across weeks. Together they are how the app remembers without measuring.

VI — Tending well

The refusal of a single metric

Tending Corpus well does not look like tending Animus well. Tending Naturum well does not even resemble tending Opus well. A uniform metric across the twelve would have to lie about most of them.

The deep reason for no dashboard

The brief refuses dashboards. The refusal is not aesthetic; it is epistemological. To measure all twelve uniformly — to render eight illegible-or-retrospective houses as numbers next to four legible ones — is to misunderstand at least eight of them. The dashboard would be a lie composed of true digits.

The substitute is not nothing. The substitute is the user, looking at the list of names, choosing. The act of choosing where to spend yourself today is itself the load-balancing mechanism. The home screen does not measure because it does not need to; it makes the twelve visible, in the right order, side by side, and trusts the person standing in the atrium to feel which door has weight today.

The list of twelve, side by side, in the right order, is the tool. Everything else — the lists, the conversations, the weekly close — is in service of keeping that list legible.

Twelve houses, twelve clocks.
Five verbs of attention.
One week to hold them all.
The atrium does not measure. It opens the doors.
