Atrium

A contemplative tool for allocating attention across twelve domains of life.

I — Premise

The open central court

The name comes from the Roman atrium — the open central court of a house, the room you pass through to reach every other room.

You open the app. You see twelve names. You choose where to spend yourself today. Inside each of the twelve: an ongoing prioritized list, and a week-long conversation with an LLM that serves as a thinking partner for that domain. That is the whole tool.

Load-balancing across a life is hard because the twelve domains are normally invisible to each other. They live in different apps, different conversations, different parts of the head. Atrium puts them side by side, by name, in a single column, and asks: which one today?

Reading the negative space
Atrium is not a dashboard. There are no vitality bars, no scores, no felt-state ratings, no inferred metrics. It does not tell you how you are doing. It is not a productivity app — no streaks, no throughput, no “you have been neglecting Polis” nudges. The LLM in each room is a thinking partner, not a coach. The load balancing emerges from the user seeing the twelve names and choosing. The app’s job is to hold the lists and the conversations with fidelity.

House view — Opus

The list

Open todos at top, ordered by manual priority. A subtle divider, then recently-completed items struck through and faded. Shelved items collapsed under a disclosure. Add manually at the top of the list. Tap to edit inline. Long-press for the item menu: done, shelve, unshelve, delete. Drag to reorder. Completed todos can be uncompleted — they fall back to the bottom of the open list.

Shelve is the default soft-delete. Delete requires confirmation. Shelve does not. Atrium is a forgiving system.

The conversation

Continuous within a week. On Sunday at 23:59 local, the week closes. Execution is lazy — nothing fires at midnight; the rollover happens the next time you open the house. At rollover the prior week is locked read-only, a 200–400 word summary is generated from the transcript and the list state, and a new empty conversation begins with the summary as a card at the top.

If a week had no messages, no summary is generated. The new week begins blank.

Inline extraction, never silent

When the LLM identifies an action-shaped item, it calls a propose_todo tool. The proposal renders inline as a small card with three buttons: Add, Edit, Skip. Nothing is added to the list without explicit confirmation. Background extraction is deliberately not supported.

meta_md — the long memory

Each house holds a user-edited markdown note that survives weekly rollover and is included in every system prompt for that house. In Vinculum it names the people who matter and how you think about them. In Opus, current projects and what success looks like. In Corpus, training goals and what fluid movement means for this body. In Patrimonium, what enough means. This field is what makes the partner genuinely useful over months rather than just a chat that resets.

IV — Aesthetic

A contemplative surface

The visual design should feel like an open room, not a packed dashboard.

  • Typography. A refined serif for display — EB Garamond or Cormorant. Mono only for metadata and structural labels. No Inter, no Roboto, no system sans.
  • Color. Warm, restrained. Vellum, parchment, ink. One muted accent used sparingly — oxblood here. Avoid bright primaries.
  • Density. Generous whitespace. The home screen, in particular, should feel like an open room.
  • Motion. Minimal. Subtle transitions only. No springs on every interaction.
  • Words over icons. Don’t reach for an icon library by reflex. A text label is usually better.

This microsite is itself a calibration of the aesthetic. The app should feel of a piece with it.

V — Decisions log

Settled

Resolved during design. Don’t re-litigate without a real reason.

  1. No dashboard. Atrium does not measure the user.
  2. No felt-state rating. Completing todos is the only signal; the user’s own choosing on the home screen is the load-balancing mechanism.
  3. Sunday night rollover, lazy execution. All houses share the same week boundary; nothing fires at midnight.
  4. Inline todo extraction with explicit confirmation. No background extraction.
  5. Per-house meta_md. Long-lived context that survives weekly rollover and rides in every system prompt.
  6. Order preserved — self to world. Don’t alphabetize. The progression is part of the form.
  7. Shelve is the default soft-delete. Delete requires confirmation. Shelve does not.
VI — Out of scope for v1

What we are not building

Resist building these into v1 even when they feel obvious.

  • Notifications, reminders, nudges of any kind.
  • Cross-house analytics — “you spent 40% of your time on Opus”.
  • Felt-state ratings, vitality scores, dashboard tiles.
  • Streaks, gamification, achievement markers.
  • Collaboration, sharing, multi-user.
  • Native mobile apps. Web, responsive.
  • Voice input. (Tempting for the conversation pane — defer to v1.5.)
VII — Building order

One useful slice at a time

Each step should be usable on its own. The MVP of the MVP is steps 1–4 — a working list-only Atrium on real data, before anything talks back.

  1. Schema + migrations. Seed the twelve houses.
  2. Home screen — the list of doors with open-todo counts.
  3. House view shell — header, two-pane layout, back link.
  4. Todo CRUD in the list pane. No LLM yet.
  5. Conversation pane with plain LLM call. No tools, no week boundary.
  6. Add the propose_todo tool and the inline confirmation card.
  7. Add per-house meta_md.
  8. Add week boundary logic and lazy rollover.
  9. Add summary generation on rollover.
  10. Aesthetic pass.
